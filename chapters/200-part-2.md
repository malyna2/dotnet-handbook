# Part 2: Middle → Senior

> **What Part 2 makes you able to do.** Explain why the platform behaves the way it does under load, keep data and messages consistent across services and over time, choose an architecture and defend its trade-offs, run what you build in production and in the cloud, build with and around AI, and make the decisions a team trusts a senior with: what to build, what to defer, and what to write down.

## How to Read Part 2

Part 2 assumes [Part 1](#part-1-junior-middle). It doesn't repeat what `await` does, why a queue redelivers or what an index seek is; each chapter goes one level down (the internals behind Part 1's rules) and one level out (the same mechanism across services, under load and over months). Read the first eight chapters in order; after that, the groups in the sidebar (cloud and Azure, AI, frontend, incidents and career, the Trusted Advisor) can be read in the order your work needs them.

Part 2 practises on labs, exercises and your own system rather than on 30-line programs: the slow-query lab, the emulator-verified Service Bus exercises, an instrumented service, an architecture decision record. Many chapters end with a **Decide** section: a situation with two or three defensible answers. Write your choice and the condition that would change it before you open the reasoning.

Your own write-ups, decisions and evidence belong in your own public portfolio repository, not in this one, and stories about a real employer stay private.

Before an interview, reread [Part 2 · Pay Attention To](#part-2-pay-attention-to).
